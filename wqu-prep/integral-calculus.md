# Integral Calculus

## THE ONLY THING YOU NEED TO KNOW

1. Integration reverses differentiation. An indefinite integral is a family of antiderivatives, so include +C.
2. Definite integrals measure signed accumulation. By the Fundamental Theorem, integral from a to b of f(x) dx = F(b)-F(a) when F'=f.
3. Match the method to the structure: substitution for an inner function and its derivative, integration by parts for products, and partial fractions for rational functions.
4. Integrals compute net change, area, average value, and volume; translate the story into a function before calculating.

## Worked-example walkthrough

Find integral from 0 to 1 of 2x(1+x^2)^3 dx. Let u=1+x^2, so du=2x dx. The bounds become u=1 and u=2. The integral is integral from 1 to 2 of u^3 du = [u^4/4] from 1 to 2 = 15/4.

## MEMORY TOOLS

### Mnemonic

**P-S-I-G**: Power rule, Substitute, Integration by parts, Geometry. Check these four lenses before choosing a method.

### Hooks

- A derivative-looking factor beside parentheses is a substitution hook.
- A product of unlike types, such as x e^x, is an integration-by-parts hook.
- Bounds mean evaluate; do not add +C.
- Integral units are integrand units multiplied by variable units.

### ASCII/text mini mind-map

```text
                         INTEGRAL
                            |
       +--------------------+--------------------+
       |                    |                    |
  antiderivative       accumulation          geometry
       |                    |                    |
  +C if indefinite     FTC: F(b)-F(a)    area/volume/average
       |
 method -> substitution | parts | partial fractions
```

### When-you-see-X-think-Y patterns

- f'(x)[f(x)]^n -> substitution.
- A product like x ln(x) or x e^x -> integration by parts.
- Integral of f'(x) from a to b -> endpoint difference f(b)-f(a).
- “Average value on [a,b]” -> (1/(b-a)) times the definite integral.
- “Total distance” -> integrate |v(t)|, not signed displacement alone.

### Common-trap warnings

- Do not forget +C on an indefinite integral.
- A definite integral can be negative or can cancel areas above and below the axis.
- In substitution, transform the bounds or substitute back before evaluating.
- The power rule does not apply to x^(-1); integral of 1/x is ln|x|+C.
- For volumes, identify outer and inner radii and square them before integrating.
